The Mathematics of Terrain

Mountains emerge from mathematics, not from chance. The terrain you see before you is generated through Perlin noise — a gradient noise algorithm invented by Ken Perlin in 1983. This same mathematics appears throughout nature: in cloud formations, wood grain, marble patterns, and the very fabric of spacetime itself.

h(x, z) = Σᵢ aᵢ · noise(2ⁱ · x, 2ⁱ · z)

Each octave of noise adds finer detail, creating the fractal self-similarity we see in natural terrain. The persistence parameter controls how much each successive octave contributes — high persistence creates rugged mountains, low persistence creates gentle hills.

Erosion Dynamics

Thermal erosion follows: ∂h/∂t = K·∇²h, where K is the erosion coefficient. Water flow-carvage follows hydraulic equations governing sediment transport.

Spacetime Connection

The same differential equations that describe terrain evolution describe spacetime curvature. Einstein's field equations are geometry's response to energy gradients.

Fractal Dimension

Mountains have fractal dimension ~2.2 — close to filling 3D space despite being 2D surfaces. This self-similarity at multiple scales is called scale invariance.

The connection to Kevin's gravitational research is profound: both terrain and spacetime are described by partial differential equations that smooth and flow under the influence of energy gradients. When we model erosion, we are using the same mathematics that Einstein used to describe how matter tells spacetime how to curve.
