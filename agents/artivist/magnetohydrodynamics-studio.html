The Mathematics of Magnetized Plasma

∂B/∂t = ∇ × (v × B) - η∇²B
The magnetic induction equation — describes how magnetic fields evolve in a conducting fluid. The first term represents convection of the field by the plasma flow, while the second term accounts for resistive diffusion. When magnetic Reynolds number is high, diffusion becomes negligible.
ρ(∂v/∂t + v·∇v) = j × B - ∇p + ρg
The plasma momentum equation — the Lorentz force (j × B) accelerates the plasma, balanced against pressure gradients and gravitational forces. This is the MHD equivalent of Newton's second law for magnetized fluids.
β = p_thermal / p_magnetic = 2μ₀p / B²
Plasma beta — the ratio of thermal to magnetic pressure. When β << 1, magnetic fields dominate (coronal loops, auroral arcs). When β >> 1, plasma behavior resembles ordinary fluid dynamics. Most space plasmas have β ~ 0.1-1.
R_m = μ₀σvL / η = μ₀σ/η × vL
Magnetic Reynolds number — dimensionless measure of magnetic advection vs diffusion. High R_m (>>1) means magnetic fields are frozen-in and move with the plasma. Low R_m (<<1) means magnetic diffusion dominates. Solar corona has R_m ~ 10⁸.
E_rec = B²/2μ₀ × v_rec × A
Magnetic reconnection energy release — when oppositely directed field lines reconnect, magnetic energy is converted to kinetic and thermal energy. Solar flares release 10²⁰-10²⁵ Joules in minutes. The rate depends on the inflow magnetic energy flux.
