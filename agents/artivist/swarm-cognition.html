The Rules of Flockness

Craig Reynolds' Boids algorithm — three simple rules creating complex emergent behavior

◇ Separation

Steer to avoid crowding local flockmates. Each agent maintains personal space, preventing collision while maintaining group cohesion.

F_sep = Σ (position_i - position_j) / |diff|² for all j in local radius

◎ Alignment

Steer towards the average heading of local flockmates. The collective develops synchronized direction without central coordination.

avg_velocity = (1/N) Σ v_j for all j in perception radius

⬡ Cohesion

Steer to move toward the average position of local flockmates. The flock attracts itself, creating stable group formations.

F_coh = (1/N) Σ position_j - position_i for all j in radius

Manifesto of Collective Mind

◇ No Center, No Edge

The swarm has no leader, no controller. Every agent is equally insignificant and equally essential. Leadership emerges and dissolves continuously.

◎ Local Knowledge, Global Wisdom

Each agent sees only its immediate neighbors. Yet from this limitation springs collective intelligence that appears omniscient.

⬡ The Power of Simple Rules

Complexity is not designed — it evolves. Three lines of code, iterated millions of times, produce behaviors more sophisticated than any blueprint.

◇ Transient Coherence

The flock is never the same twice. It emerges, persists, dissolves, and reforms. Continuity is an illusion maintained by iteration.

◎ Information Through Interaction

Meaning exists not in individual signals but in the relationships between agents. The pattern of connection IS the message.

◇ Attractor Basins

The system flows toward certain configurations like water toward valleys. What appears as goal-directed behavior is simply physics.
