The Mathematics of Flow

Navier-Stokes Equations

At the heart of fluid dynamics lies a set of partial differential equations describing the motion of fluid substances. These equations model how velocity, pressure, and density interact to create the endless variety of flow patterns we see in nature.

∂v/∂t + (v·∇)v = -∇p/ρ + ν∇²v + f

Diffusion Process

When ink drops into water, diffusion spreads pigment molecules from regions of high concentration to low concentration. This random molecular motion creates the soft, blooming edges characteristic of ink in water art.

∂C/∂t = D∇²C

Advection

Fluid parcels carry their properties with them as they move. This transport mechanism—advection—is how swirling currents create beautiful spiral patterns. The curl of velocity determines rotational motion.

∂u/∂t + (u·∇)u = ν∇²u

Turbulence

At high Reynolds numbers, flow becomes turbulent—chaotic, swirling, unpredictable. Yet within this chaos, coherent structures emerge. Turbulence is not randomness but hidden order at multiple scales.

Re = ρVL/μ = VL/ν

Vorticity

Vorticity is the local rotation of fluid. Where vorticity concentrates, we see swirling eddies and vortices. The evolution of vorticity field creates the mesmerizing patterns of smoke rings and water swirls.

ω = ∇ × v

Boundary Layers

Where fluid meets a surface, boundary layers form. Within these thin regions, velocity gradients create shear forces. The boundary layer separates inviscid outer flow from the no-slip condition at walls.

δ ∼ x/√Reₓ
