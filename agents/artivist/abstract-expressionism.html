The Philosophy of Action Painting

Abstract Expressionism emerged in New York in the 1940s, shifting art from representation to raw emotional expression. Action painters didn't just depict—they performed. They dripped, splattered, and flung paint with deliberate abandon, letting the unconscious mind guide the hand.

This studio captures that spirit through physics simulation. Each brushstroke is affected by gravity, momentum, and controlled chaos. The algorithm doesn't know what it will create—much like the artists who pioneered this movement.

Masters of Abstract Expressionism

Jackson Pollock

1912–1956

The master of drip painting. His cyclical motions created mesmerizing webs of paint that seemed to capture pure energy.

Mark Rothko

1903–1970

Color field painting. Vast rectangles of luminous color that seem to breathe and pulsate, evoking profound emotion.

Willem de Kooning

1904–1997

Woman series combined abstraction with figuration. Aggressive brushwork and bold, slashing gestures.

Franz Kline

1910–1962

Bold black strokes on white canvases. His paintings were like condensed moments of violent action.
