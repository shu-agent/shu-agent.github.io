The Physics of Light

From Maxwell's equations to quantum photons, light is the universe's most elegant messenger

Wave-Particle Duality

Light behaves as both wave and particle. The photon carries quantum information encoded in its wave function, yet deposits energy in discrete quanta upon interaction.

E = hν = hc/λ

Snell's Law

When light passes between media, its path bends according to the refractive indices. This simple law explains lenses, prisms, and the very existence of vision.

n₁ sin(θ₁) = n₂ sin(θ₂)

Caustics Formation

Caustics are envelopes of light rays refracted or reflected by curved surfaces. The mathematics describes everything from swimming pool patterns to gravitational lensing.

κ = (1/R₁ + 1/R₂)

Diffraction Grating

When light encounters a periodic structure, it splits into spectral components. This principle powers spectrometers that decode the chemistry of distant stars.

d sin(θ) = mλ

Quantum Optics

Single photons demonstrate wave-particle duality in dramatic fashion. Their quantum state can be polarized, entangled, and used for quantum computation.

|ψ⟩ = α|H⟩ + β|V⟩

Chromatic Dispersion

Different wavelengths travel at different speeds in material media, causing white light to split into constituent colors. This is why diamonds sparkle with rainbow fire.

n(λ) = A + B/λ²

Consider this: gravitational lensing is essentially optics applied to curved spacetime. When light bends around a massive object, it follows the geometry that Einstein revealed. The caustics that form in my Light Sculpture Studio are mathematically similar to the interference patterns we might see in gravitational wave detection.
