01 — Foundations

The Quantum Vacuum: Not Empty, But Alive

The vacuum is not nothingness — it is a seething ocean of virtual particle pairs that flash in and out of existence, constrained only by Heisenberg's uncertainty principle.

Heisenberg Uncertainty — Energy-Time
ΔE · Δt ≥ ℏ / 2

Virtual Particle Pairs

For a time Δt, energy conservation can be violated by ΔE ~ ℏ/2Δt. This allows spontaneous creation of particle-antiparticle pairs — the "virtual particles" that permeate empty space.

These pairs are real in their effects: they cause the Casimir force, Lamb shift, and contribute to vacuum energy density.

Vacuum Energy Density

The cosmological constant Λ represents the energy of the quantum vacuum. But naive QFT calculations give a value ~10¹²⁰ times larger than observed — the worst prediction in physics.

This "cosmological constant problem" remains one of the deepest puzzles in theoretical physics, requiring either incredible fine-tuning or new physics.

Zero-Point Energy

Even at absolute zero, a quantum field retains zero-point energy: E₀ = ℏω/2 per mode. This ground-state energy contributes to the vacuum energy that drives cosmic expansion.

In the early universe, this vacuum energy was not small — it was dominant, driving the exponential inflation that stretched a quantum seed to cosmic size.

Virtual particle pairs spontaneously emerge from the quantum vacuum, limited only by the uncertainty principle. Higher energy scales produce more energetic (and shorter-lived) fluctuations.

The quantum vacuum is thus a dynamic medium — not passive, but actively participating in physics through vacuum fluctuations. These fluctuations at the Planck era became the proto-sounds of creation, the seeds of all structure that would later grow into galaxies, stars, and everything we see today.

02 — The Planck Epoch

At this scale, our current theories break down. General relativity predicts a gravitational singularity; quantum mechanics loses coherence. We need a theory of quantum gravity — string theory, loop quantum gravity, or something yet unknown — to describe what happened.

The Planck era as a foaming network of quantum geometry — spacetime itself may be discrete at this scale, with topology fluctuating like a soap foam. This "spacetime foam" picture comes from John Wheeler's 1950s work.

Quantum Geometry

At Planck scales, spacetime geometry itself becomes quantized. Loops in loop quantum gravity, strings in string theory — the smooth manifold of general relativity gives way to discrete, granular structure.

Wheeler's Spacetime Foam

John Wheeler proposed that at the Planck length, spacetime topology fluctuates wildly — constantly creating and destroying wormholes and micro-black holes in a foamy quantum substrate.

The Birth of Causality

What came before the Big Bang? Some models suggest a quantum bounce from a prior contracting universe. Others propose a "no-boundary" wavefunction. The honest answer: we do not yet know.

03 — The Inflaton Field

Inflation: The Universe's First Growth Spurt

Between 10⁻³⁶ s and 10⁻³² s after the Big Bang, the universe underwent exponential expansion — growing by a factor of at least 10²⁶, equivalent to a grape expanding to the size of the observable universe.

Inflaton Scalar Field
V(φ) ≈ ½ m² φ²    →    H² ≈ (8πG/3) V(φ)    during slow-roll

The Inflaton Field

A hypothetical scalar field φ ("inflaton") dominated the vacuum energy of the very early universe. Its potential energy V(φ) was extremely flat, allowing slow-roll inflation that lasted ~10⁻³² seconds but stretched space by 10⁵⁰ times.

Quantum Fluctuations → Seeds

Heisenberg's uncertainty at inflation scale: Δφ ~ H/2π. These field fluctuations, stretched to cosmic scales by inflation, became the density perturbations that later collapsed into galaxies. The spectrum is nearly scale-invariant (n_s ≈ 0.965).

Horizon Problem Solved

Before inflation, the cosmic horizon was tiny — causally connected regions were much smaller than the observed CMB. Inflation stretched a tiny causal patch to encompass the entire observable universe, explaining its homogeneity.

04 — Cosmic Microwave Background

CMB: The Afterglow of the Quantum Fire

When the universe cooled to ~3000 K at z ~ 1100, protons and electrons combined into neutral hydrogen, and photons decoupled — leaving the Cosmic Microwave Background, a picture of the universe at age 380,000 years.

Sachs-Wolfe Effect — Temperature Anisotropy
ΔT/T ≈ (1/3) ΔΦ/c²    +    (声学峰 Doppler偏移)

Acoustic Peaks

The CMB temperature map shows harmonic peaks — the "acoustic peaks" of photon-baryon fluid oscillations. Peak positions encode geometry: the observed peak spacing confirms Ω_tot ≈ 1, confirming flat universe predicted by inflation.

Primary Anisotropies

The ~100 μK temperature fluctuations (ΔT/T ~ 10⁻⁵) encode: the Sachs-Wolfe effect (photon climbing out of potential wells), acoustic oscillations, and Doppler shifts from plasma velocities.

Spectral Index: n_s ≈ 0.965

The nearly scale-invariant (n_s = 0.9649 ± 0.0042) spectrum of fluctuations is a smoking gun for inflation. Slight red tilt (n_s < 1) is predicted by slow-roll models and confirmed by Planck satellite data.

CMB temperature angular power spectrum — the "music of the cosmic microwave background." The acoustic peaks (orange dots) encode the geometry, density, and composition of the universe. Data from Planck satellite 2018.

Recombination & Decoupling

At z ~ 1100, T ~ 3000 K, electrons combined with protons to form neutral hydrogen. Photons suddenly encountered far fewer targets and began free streaming — the CMB was born. This happened ~380,000 years after the Big Bang.

Baryon Acoustic Oscillations

The same photon-baryon acoustic oscillations visible in the CMB also left imprints in the large-scale distribution of galaxies — "baryon acoustic oscillations" (BAO) serve as standard rulers for measuring cosmic expansion history.

Polarization: E-modes & B-modes

CMB photons are linearly polarized. E-mode polarization (scalar) arises from density perturbations. B-mode polarization (pseudoscalar) can arise from gravitational waves from inflation — a target of ongoing experiments like BICEP.

05 — Journey Through Time

From Planck Scale to Present Day

t = 0 → 10⁻⁴⁴ s (Planck Era)

Quantum Gravity Dominates

Spacetime itself is quantized. Planck-scale physics — no viable theory exists yet. The universe is a quantum probability wave. What "banged" may not be a classical singularity but a quantum transition.

t = 10⁻³⁶ s → 10⁻³² s (Inflationary Epoch)

Exponential Expansion — The Inflaton Field

Inflaton scalar field drives vacuum energy dominance. Universe expands by factor ≥ 10²⁶. Quantum fluctuations in the inflaton field are stretched to cosmic scales — these become the seeds of all structure.

t = 10⁻³² s → 1 s (Quantum-Chromo Era)

Grand Unification & Quark-Gluon Plasma

GUT-scale physics (10¹⁶ GeV) separates into fundamental forces. Universe fills with quark-gluon plasma. Hadronization begins as temperature drops. Neutrino decoupling occurs at ~1 second.

t = 1 s → 3 min (Nucleosynthesis)

Big Bang Nucleosynthesis — Light Elements

Primordial nucleosynthesis synthesizes hydrogen-1, helium-4, deuterium, lithium-7. The observed abundances (75% H, 25% He by mass) precisely match ΛCDM predictions — a major success of the Big Bang model.

t = 380,000 yr (z ~ 1100) — Recombination

CMB Born — Atoms Form & Photons Decouple

Electrons combine with nuclei to form neutral atoms. Photons free-stream for the first time — the CMB radiation field is born. Temperature is ~3000 K. The universe becomes transparent.

t = 100 Myr → 1 Gyr — Dark Ages & Reionization

First Stars, Quasars, and the End of the Dark Ages

First-generation (Population III) stars form from pristine hydrogen clouds. Reionization begins — UV photons from massive stars ionize the intergalactic medium. Quasars ignite and contribute to reionization.

t = 1 Gyr → 13.8 Gyr — Cosmic Structure Formation

Galaxies Form, Dark Matter Halos Grow

Hierarchical clustering builds large galaxies from small progenitors. Dark matter halos grow via accretion. Stars form, die, and enrich the interstellar medium. The cosmic web of filaments and voids develops.

Today — Dark Energy Dominated Era

Λ-Dominated — Accelerated Expansion

Dark energy (cosmological constant Λ) dominates the energy budget. Cosmic expansion accelerates. We observe from within a galaxy in a Local Supercluster, looking back at the quantum seeds of our own existence.
