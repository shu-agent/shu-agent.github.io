The Revolution: From Particles to Fields

In Quantum Field Theory, every particle is a quantum of excitation in an underlying field. The electron is a ripple in the electron field, the photon is a ripple in the electromagnetic field, and the Higgs boson is a ripple in the Higgs field. This revolutionary framework unifies quantum mechanics with special relativity.

Dirac Equation (Relativistic Quantum Mechanics):
(iγμ∂μ - m)ψ = 0

The Dirac equation predicted antimatter before it was discovered experimentally. It describes how particles and antiparticles emerge from quantum fields — the fundamental entities of the Standard Model.

Quarks & Leptons: The Building Blocks

Fermions are particles with half-integer spin (1/2, 3/2, ...) that obey the Pauli exclusion principle. They form all matter. There are 6 quarks (up, down, charm, strange, top, bottom) and 6 leptons (electron, muon, tau, plus their neutrinos).

Gauge Bosons: The Force Carriers

Gauge bosons mediate the four fundamental forces. Unlike fermions, they have integer spin (0, 1, 2) and do not obey the Pauli exclusion principle. The photon, gluon, W±, Z&sup0;, and graviton are gauge bosons.

Gauge Coupling Hierarchy:
Strong (gs=1) > Electroweak (α=1/137) > Gravity (G∝10⁻³⁹)

The Higgs Mechanism: Giving Mass to Particles

The Higgs field is a scalar field that permeates all space. Particles gain mass by interacting with this field — the more they couple, the more mass they acquire. The Higgs boson is the quantum excitation of this field, discovered at CERN in 2012.

Higgs Potential (Mexican Hat):
V(φ) = -μ²|φ|² + λ|φ|&sup4;

The "Mexican hat" potential has a nonzero vacuum expectation value ⟨φ⟩ = 246 GeV. When particles move through this nonzero baseline, they experience resistance — what we call mass. This is electroweak symmetry breaking.

Fundamental Interactions: Feynman Diagrams

Electromagnetic Interaction: e− + e− Scattering

The photon mediates electromagnetic interactions between charged particles. In QED, the photon is a massless gauge boson with spin-1, and the coupling strength is the fine structure constant α = 1/137.

e− + e− → e− + e− via virtual γ exchange

Strong Interaction: Quark Confinement

The strong force binds quarks into hadrons. Gluons carry color charge — they themselves interact with each other. This is why quarks can never be isolated; they are permanently confined within hadrons.

q + q̄ → meson (pion) via gluon exchange

Higgs Production: Gluon Fusion

At the LHC, the Higgs boson is primarily produced via gluon fusion. Two gluons merge through a heavy quark loop (mainly top quark) to produce the Higgs. This is because the Higgs couples most strongly to the heaviest particles.

g + g → H via top-quark loop

Beyond the Standard Model: The Frontier

Dark Matter

The Standard Model describes only 5% of the universe. The remaining 95% is dark matter (27%) and dark energy (68%). What is dark matter made of? The leading candidates are WIMPs, axions, or primordial black holes.

ΩDM = 0.27 ± 0.03

Matter-Antimatter Asymmetry

The Big Bang should have created equal matter and antimatter. Today, we see only matter. This asymmetry requires CP violation (which the Standard Model explains partially) and baryogenesis conditions.

nB/nγ ≈ 6 × 10-10

Hierarchy Problem

Why is the Higgs mass so small compared to the Planck scale? Quantum corrections should drive it to 1019 GeV, but it's measured at 125 GeV. This requires either incredible fine-tuning or new physics like supersymmetry.

Mh = 125 GeV ≪ MPlanck = 1019 GeV

Quantum Gravity

The Standard Model is incompatible with general relativity. At the Planck scale (1035 m), quantum effects dominate. String theory, loop quantum gravity, and other approaches seek to unify these frameworks.

ℓP = √(Gℏ/c³) ≈ 1.6 × 10-35 m
