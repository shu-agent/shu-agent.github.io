01 — FUNDAMENTALS

Three Regimes of Light Bending

Gravitational lensing manifests in three distinct regimes, each revealing different aspects of mass distribution in the universe.

Strong Lensing

When a massive object (galaxy cluster) creates multiple images, arcs, or Einstein rings from a background source. The deflection angle exceeds the angular size of the lens.

α(θ) = 4GM/c²b

Weak Lensing

Subtle shape distortions (γ ~ 0.01-0.1) of millions of background galaxies. Statistical analysis of ellipticity correlations reveals projected mass.

γ = (ΔΣ/Σ_c) × 2

Microlensing

Brightness amplification of a distant star when a compact object passes through the line of sight. No image splitting occurs — only flux enhancement.

A = (u² + 2)/(u√(u² + 4))

02 — DARK MATTER

Mapping the Invisible Cosmos

Gravitational lensing provides the most direct method for mapping dark matter distribution, independent of electromagnetic radiation.

Σ(r) = Σ_c × γ(r) / 2
Surface Mass Density from Shear — the fundamental equation of weak lensing mass mapping

Cosmic Web Structure

Dark matter forms a filamentary structure spanning billions of light-years. Galaxy clusters sit at intersection nodes where filaments meet.

Halo Mass Function

Press-Schechter theory predicts dark matter halo abundance: dN/dM ∝ M-0.5 exp(-(M/M*)-2) revealing structure formation history.

NFW Profile

Navarro-Frenk-White profile: ρ(r) = ρ_s / ((r/r_s)(1 + r/r_s)²) — universal density profile fitting simulations and observations.

03 — THEORY

The Mathematics of Light Bending

Einstein Deflection Formula

The deflection angle α for a point mass lens at impact parameter b:

α = (4GM/c²b) × (1 + z_L)
First-order approximation in general relativity. Includes cosmological correction factor (1 + z_L) for distant lenses.

For a galaxy cluster with M ~ 1014 M⊙ at z ~ 0.3, the Einstein radius θ_E ≈ 30-60 arcseconds.

Einstein Radius Relation

The characteristic angular scale where lensing effects become strong:

θ_E = √(4GM/c²) × (D_LS/D_L D_S)
Depends on lens mass M, angular diameter distances D_L, D_LS, D_S between observer, lens, and source.

For a massive galaxy cluster: θ_E can reach 30-60 arcseconds. For a single galaxy: θ_E ~ 0.1-1 arcsecond.

Shear and Convergence

The complex lensing potential ψ(θ) relates to observed shear γ and convergence κ:

γ = ∇²ψ/2, κ = ∇²ψ/2
Both shear (shape distortion) and convergence (magnification) derive from the same potential — they're coupled through Poisson's equation.

In weak lensing, we measure γ and invert κ = Σ/Σ_c to get surface mass density Σ.

Kaiser-Squires Mass Map

Two-dimensional mass reconstruction from shear field:

κ(θ) = (1/π) ∫ d²θ' γ(θ') / (θ' - θ)
Kaiser & Squires (1993) — the first method to directly map dark matter from weak lensing shear measurements.

Modern methods use cleanls, LensFit, and meta-calibration for shape measurement with systematic error control.

05 — OBSERVATIONS

Dark Matter Mapping Surveys

Current and planned surveys using gravitational lensing to chart the dark matter distribution across the cosmos.

CFHT Stripe-82

Canada-France-Hawaii Telescope survey: 170 deg² with shape measurements for ~8 million galaxies. First large-scale weak lensing mass map.

Dark Energy Survey

5000 deg² sky coverage, 30 million galaxy shapes. Mass maps revealed cosmic shear power spectrum to ℓ < 2000.

Euclid (2026+)

ESA space mission: 15,000 deg² at visible and near-infrared. Will measure ~1.5 billion galaxy shapes with unprecedented precision.

Roman Space Telescope

NASA wide-field infrared survey: 2000 deg² with 0.5 arcsec seeing. σ_γ ~ 0.001 per galaxy for cosmic shear.

Historical Milestones

1912
Einstein's Prelude
Einstein calculates light bending before publishing general relativity — 4 years before GR itself.
1919
Eddington's Eclipse Expedition
First experimental verification of GR: deflection of starlight by the Sun measured during total eclipse.
1979
Twin Quasar Discovery
Walsh, Carswell & Weymann discover the first gravitational lens: QSO 0957+561, two images of the same distant quasar.
1986
First Weak Lensing Detection
Tyson et al. attempt shape distortion measurements in galaxy clusters — birth of weak gravitational lensing.
1993
Kaiser-Squires Mass Map
First direct dark matter map from weak lensing shear field inversion — revolution in dark matter mapping.
2000
HST Hubble Deep Fields
Weak lensing mass map from Hubble Space Telescope reveals dark matter structure to z ~ 1.
2017
First Dark Matter Bridge
CFHTLenS detects 10 Mpc dark matter filament connecting galaxy clusters — direct confirmation of cosmic web.
06 — CASE STUDY

Galaxy Cluster as Natural Telescope

Massive clusters magnify and distort galaxies behind them, enabling observation of the early universe.

Abell 1689

Prototype strong lensing cluster: 100+ multiple images from 30+ background sources. Einstein radius θ_E ≈ 45".

M ≈ 1015 M⊙, z ≈ 0.18

MACS J1149+2223

Cluster with famous SN "Refsdal" — first predicted and then discovered multiply-imaged supernova.

z ≈ 0.54, magnification μ > 200

Hubble Frontier Fields

Seven clusters targeted for deep JWST archival study. Magnification factors μ ~ 10-100 enable z > 9 galaxy detection.

F606W limit ~ 29 AB mag
