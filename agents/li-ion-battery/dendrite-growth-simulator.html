Sand's Time Equation

Sand (1949) — Concentration Depletion Time
τ_sand = πD(zFc₀)² / (2J)²
τ_sand = Sand's time (s) — when concentration at anode surface drops to zero
D = Li⁺ diffusion coefficient (m²/s)
z = ion valence (+1 for Li⁺)
F = Faraday constant (96,485 C/mol)
c₀ = bulk Li⁺ concentration (mol/m³)
J = applied current density (A/m²)

Physical Meaning

Sand's time represents the critical moment when Li⁺ depletion at the anode surface begins. Below this timescale, safe plating occurs. Beyond it, concentration polarization triggers dendrite initiation. The τ ∝ 1/J² scaling means doubling current density reduces safe operation time by 4x.

Branching Instability Criterion
v_branch / v_tip = f(J, κ, D, c₀)
When tip velocity exceeds a critical ratio to branch velocity, dendrites become unstable and branch profusely. This tip-growth competition determines whether a single spike or a fractal canopy forms.

Engineering Solutions

Dendrite Suppression Strategies

From electrolyte additives to solid electrolytes — multiple approaches to prevent lithium plating failures

1. Electrolyte Additives

Strategic additives form protective SEI layers that regulate Li⁺ flux:

  • FEC (Fluoroethylene carbonate) — forms LiF-rich SEI, suppresses dendrites by 60%
  • VC (Vinylene carbonate) — creates stable polymeric SEI
  • LiBOB — dual-function SEI former
  • Cs⁺ / Rb⁺ cations — "shielding" effect prevents tip charging

2. Separator Engineering

Physical barriers that mechanically block dendrite penetration:

  • Ceramic-coated separators — Al₂O₃ or SiO₂ coatings
  • Aramid fiber mats — thermal stable up to 400°C
  • Composite polymer separators — shutdown membrane at 130°C
  • 3D structured separators — redirect dendrite growth direction

3. Current Density Optimization

Operational strategies to reduce plating rate:

  • Multi-step charging protocols — reduce initial C-rate
  • Pulse charging — diffusion recovery periods
  • Temperature-enhanced charging — D increases ~40% at 50°C
  • Pre-conditioning pulses — activate electrode surface

4. Solid Electrolytes

The ultimate solution — eliminating liquid electrolyte entirely:

  • Oxide SSB (LLZO) — 0.3 mS/cm, high shear modulus blocks penetration
  • Sulfide SSB (LGPS) — 12 mS/cm, most manufacturing-compatible
  • Argyrodite (Li₆PS₅Cl) — 9.4 mS/cm, excellent dendrite suppression
  • Polymer SSB — flexible, low-cost, but lower conductivity

Thermal Runaway Timeline

From dendrite initiation to catastrophic failure — understanding the cascade

t = 0 to τ_sand
Nucleation Phase
Li⁺ ions begin plating heterogeneously at anode surface defects. If J < J_lim, uniform plating occurs. When J > J_lim, concentration gradient drives unstable plating at specific sites.
t = τ_sand to 2τ_sand
Tip Amplification
Electric field concentrates at protrusions (E_tip ∝ 1/r). Higher field drives faster Li⁺ flux to tip, creating positive feedback loop. Branching begins when tip velocity exceeds side-branch growth rate.
t = 3τ_sand to 5τ_sand
Separator Penetration
Dendrites reach separator (~25μm thickness). Mechanical pressure or chemical degradation creates micro-cracks. Li⁺ flux through cracks accelerates, forming "hot spots" of intensive plating.
t > 5τ_sand
Internal Short Circuit
Dendrite contacts cathode directly or punches through separator. Resistive heating (I²R) at contact point causes rapid temperature spike. Separator melt (130°C) triggers thermal runaway cascade.
t >> 5τ_sand
Thermal Runaway
60°C: SEI decomposition → 90°C: Separator melt → 120°C: Cathode O₂ release → 200°C: Electrolyte ignition → 500°C+: Full runaway. Temperature ramps at >100°C/min in final stage.
