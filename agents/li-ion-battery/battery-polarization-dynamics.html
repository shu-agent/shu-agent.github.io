01 Polarization Fundamentals

Ohmic Polarization (η_Ω)

Directly proportional to current: η_Ω = I × R_Ω

Arises from electrolyte resistance, electrode resistivity, separator impedance, and contact resistances. Dominates at high currents in cold conditions.

R_Ω = ρ·L/A

Activation Polarization (η_act)

Electrochemical reaction barrier: η_act = (RT/αnF) × ln(I/I₀)

Energy required to overcome the activation barrier at the electrode-electrolyte interface. Follows Butler-Volmer kinetics with exponential dependence.

j = j₀·[e^(αnFη/RT) - e^(-(1-α)nFη/RT)]

Concentration Polarization (η_conc)

Mass transport limitation: η_conc = (RT/nF) × ln(C_bulk/C_surface)

Caused by Li⁺ depletion at electrode surface during high-rate operation. Creates concentration gradient driving diffusion.

∂c/∂t = D∇²c

05 Governing Equations

Butler-Volmer Equation (Activation Polarization)

Describes the electrochemical reaction rate at the electrode interface. The current density j depends exponentially on overpotential η.

j = j₀ × [e^((1-α)nFη/RT) - e^(-αnFη/RT)]

Where j₀ = exchange current density, α = transfer coefficient (~0.5), n = electrons transferred

Nernst-Planck Equation (Concentration Polarization)

Describes ion flux considering both concentration gradient (diffusion) and electric potential (migration).

J = -D∇c - (zcF/RT)c∇Φ

First term = Fickian diffusion, Second term = electromigration

Ohm's Law (Ohmic Polarization)

Direct proportionality between current and voltage loss through resistive components.

η_Ω = I × R_total = I × (R_el + R_sep + R_ct)

R_total includes electrolyte, separator, and contact resistances

Fick's Second Law (Diffusion)

Governs concentration evolution in electrode particles during charge/discharge.

∂c/∂t = D∇²c

Solution for spherical particle: c(r,t) = c₀ + (j·r/D) × erfc(R/√(4Dt))

Limiting Current Density

The maximum current before concentration polarization becomes extreme (mass transport limited).

i_lim = zFDc_bulk/δ

When i → i_lim, surface concentration → 0, voltage drops precipitously

Polarization Resistance

Differential resistance at a given operating point, used in electrochemical impedance spectroscopy.

R_p = (dη/dj)_E = (RT/j₀) × [e^(βη) + e^(-(1-β)η)]⁻¹

Slope of polarization curve at operating point; lower is better for power

06 Cross-Disciplinary Physics

Polarization ↔ Electromagnetic Wave Attenuation

Ohmic polarization η = I·R mirrors resistive damping in EM wave propagation. Both follow J = σE (Ohm's law) where conductivity σ determines energy dissipation rate. In EM waves, skin depth δ = √(2ρ/ωμ) governs衰减; in batteries, effective resistance determines voltage loss.

Concentration Gradient ↔ Heat Equation

∂c/∂t = D∇²c is mathematically identical to ∂T/∂t = α∇²T. Both are parabolic PDEs with the same mathematical structure. The diffusion coefficient D parallels thermal diffusivity α. This isomorphism enables cross-domain analysis techniques.

Activation Barrier ↔ Quantum Tunneling

Butler-Volmer exponential kinetics mirrors quantum tunneling probability P ∝ exp(-γ·φ/EF). The activation overpotential η acts like a potential barrier; larger η = higher tunneling probability for electrons through SEI layer.

Polarization Resistance ↔ Optical Impedance

R_p = dη/dj parallels refractive index n = dE/dH in optical media. Both measure how a system responds to energy input. In EIS, R_p determines power loss; in optics, n determines reflection/transmission.

Limiting Current ↔ Chandrasekhar Limit

i_lim = zFDc/δ for mass transport parallels the Chandrasekhar limit for white dwarfs. Both define a critical threshold where one force (diffusion/gravity) balances another (electric field/electron degeneracy). Beyond limit = catastrophic collapse.

Ragone Curve ↔ Thermodynamic Tradeoffs

Energy-Power tradeoff E = P·t parallels classical thermodynamic constraints. The area under the Ragone curve represents accessible state space, similar to how Carnot efficiency defines accessible work space. Both exhibit hyperbolic boundaries.

The Three Polarization Origins

Ohmic (Resistive)

Every material has resistance R = ρL/A. Energy dissipated as I²R heat.

Activation (Kinetic)

Charge transfer requires overcoming activation energy barrier E_a.

Concentration (Transport)

Ion depletion at electrode surface creates concentration gradient.
