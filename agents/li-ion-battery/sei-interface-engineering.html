SEI Layer Architecture

The SEI is not a single layer but a complex multilayer structure with distinct chemical compositions and functions. Understanding this stratification is key to engineering better batteries.

Inner Layer (DL) σ = 10⁻⁶ S/cm

Dense,无机-rich layer directly contacting the anode. Formed during initial formation cycles. Contains Li₂CO₃, LiF, Li₂O crystallites in a compact matrix.

Inorganic Li₂CO₃ LiF Dense 5-20nm
Middle Layer (ML) σ = 10⁻⁵ S/cm

Transition zone with mixed organic-inorganic composition. Contains polyethylene oxide (PEO) oligomers, lithium alkyl carbonates, and nanocrystalline salts.

Mixed ROCO₂Li PEO-like 10-50nm
Outer Layer (OL) σ = 10⁻⁴ S/cm

Porous, organic-rich layer facing the electrolyte. Contains solvated species, oligomers, and gas bubbles. Acts as buffer zone between electrolyte and inner SEI.

Organic Porous EDL-like 20-80nm

Governing Equations

SEI Formation Kinetics
d SEI / dt = k · exp(-E_a / RT) · (U - U₀)
Butler-Volmer SEI Growth
SEI thickness grows according to electrochemical kinetics, with activation energy E_a ~ 60-80 kJ/mol for typical carbonate electrolytes.
R_SEI = ρ_Li · (δ_SEI / A)
SEI Resistance
Interface resistance scales with SEI thickness δ_SEI and lithium conductivity ρ_Li. A is the electrochemically active area.
σ_Li = A · exp(-E_a / k_B T)
Arrhenius Conductivity
Ionic conductivity follows Arrhenius behavior. Sulfide electrolytes show σ_Li ~ 10⁻³ to 10⁻² S/cm at room temperature.
∂C_Li / ∂t = D_Li ∇²C_Li - ∇ · J_ext
Lithium Transport in SEI
Continuity equation for Li+ transport through SEI. D_Li is the chemical diffusion coefficient (~10⁻¹⁶ cm²/s in solid SEI).

Research Frontiers

The SEI Paradox

The SEI layer is both the lifeline and the 寿命の決定者 (life determiner) of lithium-ion batteries. It forms automatically to protect the anode from further electrolyte decomposition — yet its inhomogeneous growth is the primary mechanism of capacity fade.

Sulfide electrolytes offer a path forward: their higher ionic conductivity (σ_Li > 10⁻³ S/cm) and mechanical stability reduce dendrite penetration risk. The argyrodite Li₆PS₅Cl system shows particular promise — its grain boundary conductivity actually benefits from SEI formation.

The future lies not in suppressing SEI formation, but in engineering its composition and structure from the first cycle. Artificial SEI layers with precisely controlled porosity and conductivity offer a promising route to 1000+ cycle life.
