Governing Equations — Intercalation Physics

Fick's Second Law (1D Diffusion)
∂c/∂t = D ∇²c = D · ∂²c/∂x²
Li+ concentration evolves through diffusion. D = 10⁻¹⁴ to 10⁻¹² m²/s in layered oxides.
Butler-Volmer Charge Transfer
j = j₀ · [e^((1-α)Fη/RT) − e^(-αFη/RT)]
Interfacial Li+ flux depends on overpotential η and transfer coefficient α ≈ 0.5.
Diffusion Time Constant
τ = r²/D
Time for concentration wave to penetrate particle radius r. τ ∝ r² for diffusion-limited kinetics.
Shrinking Core Model
1 - (c/c₀)^(2/3) = k·t
As Li+ enters, core remains lithiated while reaction front moves inward. Valid for phase-changing materials.
Nernst Potential
E = E₀ + (RT/F) · ln([Li⁺]_surface)
Equilibrium potential depends on surface Li+ activity. Explains voltage slope during intercalation.
Arrhenius Temperature Dependence
D = D₀ · exp(-Ea/RT)
Diffusivity increases exponentially with temperature. Ea ≈ 20-40 kJ/mol for NMC.

Key Transport Phenomena

1Concentration Polarization

During high-rate cycling, Li+ consumption at the electrode surface exceeds diffusion replenishment. Surface concentration drops, causing voltage sag and capacity loss at high C-rates.

Δc_surface = I · δ / (zFD)

2Solid-State Diffusion Limitation

Li+ must diffuse through the electrode particle lattice. For spherical particles, the characteristic time τ = r²/D means 10μm particles have ~100x longer diffusion time than 1μm particles.

τ = (1 μm)²/D ≈ 1s; τ = (10 μm)²/D ≈ 100s

3Reaction Front Propagation

In the shrinking core model, lithiation proceeds radially inward. The unreacted core shrinks as Li+ penetrates. This creates distinct concentration zones visible in X-ray tomography.

r_core(t) = r₀ · (1 - k·t)^(1/3)

4Multi-Step Intercalation

Li+ insertion involves: (1) desolvation at surface, (2) electron transfer, (3) lattice incorporation. Each step has its own activation energy and rate constant.

k_total = (Σ k_i⁻¹)⁻¹ (series resistance analogy)

5Stage Transitions

Li-intercalated compounds show discrete stage phases (Stage 1, 2, 3, 4). Each stage represents a specific Li:host ratio, creating voltage plateaus as the stage front moves.

Stage n: Li_x with 1/(n+1) sites filled

6Stress Generation

Li+ insertion causes lattice expansion (NMC: ~16% c-lattice expansion). Non-uniform intercalation creates stress gradients, potentially causing particle fracture and capacity fade.

ΔV/V₀ = β · Δc (Vegard's law)

Cross-Disciplinary Physics Connections

Diffusion ↔ Heat Equation

Fick's second law ∂c/∂t = D∇²c is mathematically identical to the heat equation ∂T/∂t = α∇²T. Both are parabolic PDEs with identical solution structures. Concentration waves propagate like thermal waves.

Diffusion Length ↔ Skin Depth

Characteristic diffusion length L = √(Dt) parallels electromagnetic skin depth δ = √(2ρ/ωμ). Both follow √t or 1/√ω scaling, showing universal transport physics across domains.

Shrinking Core ↔ Melting Ice Ball

The shrinking reaction front in intercalation mirrors the Stephan problem in melting ice or alloy solidification. The moving boundary condition creates similar mathematical challenges.

Concentration Gradient ↔ Gravitational Potential

Li+ concentration gradients create electrochemical potential gradients. Similarly, mass density gradients in astrophysics create gravitational potential wells — the Poisson equation ∇²Φ = 4πGρ connects them.

Stage Transition ↔ Cosmological Phase Separation

Li staging in graphite (Stage 2 → Stage 1) resembles phase separation in early universe symmetry breaking. Both involve discrete domain formation with sharp interfaces moving through the material.

Intercalation Waves ↔ Traffic Flow

Concentration waves propagating through electrode particles follow the same kinematic wave equations as traffic flow. Wave speed v = dE/dc parallels traffic jam propagation speed.
