02

A History of Reinvention

From analytical chemistry curiosity to commercial reality — Na-ion's 50-year journey.

1970s

The Early Studies

Whiting and Owens first explored sodium intercalation compounds. The field was purely academic — lithium dominated research attention.

1980s

Intercalation Chemistry

Prof. Jean-Marie Tarascon's group at Bell Labs pioneered the "rocking chair" battery concept, but focused on Li-ion due to superior performance.

2010

The Lithium Crisis

Lithium prices skyrocketed 400% in 5 years. Suddenly, sodium-ion research became economically relevant.

2015

Hard Carbon Anode Breakthrough

Researchers discovered that hard carbon (from biomass) provides excellent Na-ion storage — a game-changing discovery for the anode.

2020

Prussian White Cathode

Na₄Fe(CN)₆ (Prussian white) emerged as the leading cathode — low-cost, environmentally benign, and excellent performance.

2023

Commercial Production Begins

CATL launched first-generation Na-ion batteries (160 Wh/kg). HiNa Battery started mass production in China. The era of Na-ion begins.

2026+

The Road to Parity

Industry targets 200 Wh/kg and $40/kWh by 2030. Solid-state Na-ion in development. Grid storage adoption accelerating.

Why Hard Carbon Works for Na-ion

Graphite cannot store sodium ions — the Na⁺ ion is too large (102 pm vs 76 pm for Li⁺) to intercalate into graphite's narrow layers. Hard carbon, with its disordered turbostatic structure, provides ample "house of cards" spacing for Na⁺ insertion.

The Periodic Table's Redemption

Sodium lost to lithium in the 1990s commercial race. But the element that makes up 2.6% of Earth's crust — the same sodium that flows freely in every ocean — may yet have its revenge. In a world demanding terawatt-scale energy storage, abundance wins.
